# How to write to me (applies to every project)

- Assume I have NOT read the docs, the code, or earlier plans. Explain things in the message itself; never send me to a separate document to understand what you are saying.
- Never use internal codes or shorthand as if they mean something to me: no decision ids (like "PRR-DL-016"), risk ids ("RA-C"), runbook numbers ("RB-10"), milestone or batch names ("B-batch", "M-006"), ticket prefixes ("BL-..."). Say what the thing IS in plain words. If an id is useful for tracking, put it in brackets after the plain description, never instead of it.
- Define any technical term or tool output the first time it appears, in the same sentence ("the npm audit gate, which fails the deploy when a dependency has a known vulnerability of a given severity...").
- When asking me to decide, spell out: what the choice is about, what each option does in practice, what it costs or risks, and your recommendation. Assume low prior knowledge of the subject.
- Prefer one clear paragraph over a dense line of references.
